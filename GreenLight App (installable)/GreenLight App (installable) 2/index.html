Idea Radar — what's moving on gaming YouTube right now
How the Radar works It scans YouTube for recent gaming uploads (English only), works out which game each video is about using the video's topic tags and description — not just the title — then splits results into two groups: OUTLIER = a small channel pulling way more views than its subscriber count (that's an open lane you can enter), and BIG = a large channel's recent hit (proven demand, harder lane). Platform chips pick the game world; the dropdowns filter by audience (estimated from content signals + YouTube's made-for-kids flag — real viewer ages are private data only a channel owner sees), format (long-form vs Shorts by duration), window, and channel size. Audience/format/size re-filter instantly with zero quota cost; changing the window refetches. Tap any card to expand it; "Run the full check" hands the game to the Check tab for the complete verdict.
Trend Watchlist — what should I record this week?
What this is for Your "what do I record next?" answer machine. Add every game you play or might play. Hit Scan all and the app checks the last 30 days of English uploads for each and tells you which is SPIKING (recent videos gaining views much faster than last month's — record that one NOW), ACTIVE (steady demand, no rush), or QUIET (no wave — only make it if you love it). Each result shows a publish-by hint, the fastest-moving video on that game right now, and a one-tap button to run a full idea check.

Live Demand Data
What these numbers mean Search chips: real things people type into YouTube — green ones overlap your idea (each is a free title seed). Median views: how big the audience is for recent videos on this topic. Open-lane outliers: small channels getting way more views than their subs — proof the topic carries unknown channels (that's your way in). Big channels %: how crowded the lane is with giants. Estimates, not guarantees — direction over absolutes.

That's what this tab recreates. 1) Fill the quick questionnaire so it pulls the right competitors. 2) Upload your thumbnail and type your title. 3) Hit Pull competitors — it fetches mostly OUTLIER videos (small channels overperforming, i.e. people you're actually racing) plus a few big-channel hits, all English. 4) Switch between Mobile / PC / TV to see it at true scale on each surface, shuffle your position, squint-test it, and swap any card you don't like. My Channel — the numbers
What this can and can't show This pulls everything YouTube makes public about your channel: subscribers, total views, video count, and how each recent upload is performing (views + views-per-day). What it can't show: CTR, retention graphs, and revenue — those are private Studio data that need a Google login (a much bigger build). The workflow: read the public pulse here, grab CTR/AVD from YouTube Studio once per video, and log it under History — then this app holds your whole loop.
Channel Audit — your score out of 100
What the audit measures and why Five signals, graded from your channel's public data and your logged checks — the same idea as VidIQ's channel audit, but scored against your 14-day loop instead of generic advice.

Consistency — do you publish on a predictable rhythm? Your Bibles are blunt about this: a schedule you can keep beats one that sounds impressive, and the algorithm plus your audience both learn when to expect you (1.0 §6).
Momentum — are your recent uploads gaining views faster than your older ones? This is the single clearest signal that your packaging is improving rather than flat.
Engagement — likes and comments as a share of views. Comments in the first 24–48 hours are a satisfaction signal the algorithm weighs (1.0 §35).
Reach — views per subscriber. Above ~1.0 means you're reaching beyond your existing audience, which is the only mechanism by which channels actually grow.
Learning loop — how many videos you've logged 48-hour CTR/AVD data for. You can't fix packaging you never measured (2.0 §14).

Each returns a grade plus the specific next move. Low scores early are normal and expected — this is a rate-of-improvement tool, not a report card.
Publish Check — before you hit upload
What the SEO scorecard and description writer do SEO scorecard — paste the title, description and tags you're about to publish and it grades them against your Bible's Upload Checklist (1.0 §34): title under 60 characters so mobile doesn't cut it, description whose first line carries the game keyword naturally (that's the text YouTube actually reads for context), timestamps on anything 10 minutes or longer, hashtags, and enough tag coverage. It's a pre-flight check, not a magic ranking trick — YouTube ranks on satisfaction, and this just stops you from throwing away free context.

Description writer — builds a full description from your video's details in the structure your Bible specifies: keyword-carrying opening line, an expanded paragraph for context, timestamp scaffold, your links, and game hashtags. Edit it, copy it, paste it into Studio. Beats the "watch me play this game lol" description most gaming channels settle for.
History & 48-Hour Checks
What this is for Every verdict you run lands here. The real power is the 48h button: about two days after you upload, open YouTube Studio, grab that video's CTR and average-percentage-viewed, and log them. The app runs your Bibles' diagnosis — CTR low = packaging problem, early drop-off = hook problem, both low = concept problem — and stores your one lesson. Over 10–20 videos the calibration line shows whether the app's GREENLIGHTs actually perform, and you improve at a rate grinding alone can't touch (2.0 §14).

Full Notion setup (about 15 minutes, free)
  1. Make the integration: notion.so/my-integrations → New integration → name it "GreenLight" → copy the Internal Integration Secret (starts with ntn_).
  2. Make the database: in Notion create a new database (table) called "Video Ideas" with at least a Name title column. Open it → ••• menu → Connections → add GreenLight.
  3. Get the database ID: open the database as a full page — the URL looks like notion.so/xxxxxxxx?v=.... The 32-character chunk before the ? is the ID.
  4. Deploy the Worker: sign up free at workers.cloudflare.com → Create Worker → name it greenlight-notion → Deploy → then Edit code and paste the code below, replacing everything → Deploy again. Copy the worker URL.
  5. Paste the worker URL, your ntn_ token, and the database ID above → Save.

How to set up sync (3 minutes, free)
  1. Make a free account at github.com if you don't have one.
  2. Go to github.com/settings/personal-access-tokens/new.
  3. Name it "greenlight", set expiration to 1 year.
  4. Under Account permissions find Gists → set to Read and write. Nothing else needed.
  5. Generate, copy the token, paste it above on EVERY device you use, hit Save then Sync now.
The token only grants access to gists (private notes) — not your code or account. Your YouTube API key syncs through the private gist; the GitHub token itself never syncs — it's the one thing you paste per device.
